# COMPLIANCE, PROVENANCE AND RIGHTS CONTRACT

Status: PREPARATION ONLY. This document defines responsibility boundaries and evidence expectations. It does not encode final legal conclusions, category-specific rules, provider policies or schema names.

## 1. Core rule

NO PRODUCT MAY BECOME BUYER-SELLABLE MERELY BECAUSE PRODUCT DATA CAN BE FETCHED.

The canonical path is:

SOURCE
→ IDENTIFY
→ PROVENANCE
→ RIGHTS
→ PRODUCT FACTS
→ CATEGORY/RISK CLASSIFICATION
→ COMPLIANCE EVIDENCE
→ REVIEW/POLICY DECISION
→ COMMERCIAL ELIGIBILITY
→ PUBLISH/SELLABILITY.

Fetching data is not evidence of a right to reuse it, sell the item, import it, advertise it or make regulated claims.

## 2. Separate responsibilities

The platform must distinguish at minimum:

- source provenance;
- content/image usage rights;
- product identity evidence;
- product compliance evidence;
- supplier qualification evidence;
- territory eligibility;
- category restrictions;
- buyer-facing claims;
- operational review decisions;
- evidence freshness/expiry.

None of these should be collapsed into one generic `approved=true` concept.

## 3. Provenance

For every imported/sourced product candidate, preserve enough evidence to answer:

- where did this data come from;
- when was it observed/imported;
- by what connector/import method;
- what source reference identifies the source item;
- which fields came directly from source data;
- which fields were normalized;
- which fields were generated by AI;
- which fields were manually edited;
- what evidence supports material facts;
- what source freshness applies.

Provenance is append-safe evidence. Editing buyer-facing content must not erase the original source/evidence trail.

## 4. Rights / content reuse

The platform must not assume that availability of:

- product images;
- titles;
- descriptions;
- specifications;
- videos;
- reviews;
- marketplace content;

means Loadify has a right to republish them.

For each source/provider, current official terms and contractual permissions must be verified before provider-specific implementation.

Possible outcomes at policy level may include:

- permitted reuse;
- permitted factual extraction only;
- supplier-provided licensed media only;
- manual replacement media required;
- no reuse;
- unclear/review required.

Do not copy third-party reviews into Loadify product truth merely because they are visible externally.

## 5. Product factual claims

Claims that can materially affect customer safety, regulatory status, suitability or purchasing decision require evidence.

Examples include:

- dimensions;
- materials;
- origin;
- manufacturer;
- ingredients;
- certifications;
- warranty;
- safety claims;
- electrical ratings;
- compatibility;
- medical/health claims;
- age restrictions;
- hazardous/restricted classification;
- food/cosmetic/product-contact attributes.

AI Product Builder may rephrase verified facts but must not promote inference into fact.

## 6. Evidence classes

Preparation model only; final implementation names come after Gate B.

Evidence should be distinguishable by strength/source, for example:

- supplier declaration;
- manufacturer documentation;
- regulatory/certification document;
- trusted catalog/feed;
- official API response;
- operator-entered evidence;
- source page observation;
- machine-extracted candidate fact;
- AI-generated wording derived from verified facts.

An AI-generated sentence is never itself evidence of the underlying fact.

## 7. Category/risk classification

Before publish/sellability, product candidates must pass a category/risk classification step sufficient to determine what evidence/policy is required.

Potential classifications may include:

- ordinary low-risk goods;
- age-restricted goods;
- regulated electrical/electronic goods;
- toys/children's products;
- cosmetics/personal care;
- food/contact products;
- medical/health-related goods;
- batteries/hazardous transport;
- branded/counterfeit/IP-sensitive goods;
- other restricted/high-risk categories.

This list is not a final legal taxonomy. Current UK rules must be checked from official sources before implementation.

## 8. Supplier compliance responsibility

Supplier qualification must establish what the supplier can credibly provide and what Loadify requires from that supplier for each category/territory.

Possible evidence responsibilities:

- business identity;
- manufacturer/brand relationship;
- product authenticity;
- compliance declarations/documents;
- recall cooperation;
- traceability;
- batch/lot/serial support where relevant;
- returns/recovery support;
- incident response;
- territory restrictions.

Supplier qualification does not automatically approve every supplier offer.

## 9. Offer-level compliance

ONE SUPPLIER MAY HAVE MANY OFFERS WITH DIFFERENT COMPLIANCE/RISK STATES.

Therefore supplier-approved ≠ offer-approved.

Offer eligibility may depend on:

- canonical product compliance state;
- supplier-specific evidence;
- territory;
- source rights;
- freshness;
- recall/incident state;
- supplier risk;
- product/variant-specific restrictions.

## 10. Recall / incident handling

The architecture must be able to respond to a product safety/compliance incident without deleting history.

Required responsibility path:

INCIDENT DETECTED
→ identify affected canonical product/variant/offers/orders
→ stop sellability where required
→ preserve evidence/history
→ identify customer/order exposure
→ supplier/provider escalation
→ customer/admin action plan
→ financial/return implications
→ resolution/closure evidence.

A kill switch may stop new commerce but must not remove historical records needed for investigation.

## 11. Content and image replacement

If imported content lacks sufficient rights or provenance, operator workflow must allow:

- removal of source media;
- upload of licensed/original media;
- re-authoring descriptions from verified facts;
- manual compliance review;
- publish only after evidence is sufficient.

The canonical product identity should not depend on copying an external marketplace's merchandising.

## 12. Auditability

Every significant compliance/provenance decision should be attributable to:

- evidence version/reference;
- decision/rule version;
- actor/system;
- timestamp;
- outcome;
- reason;
- expiry/review date where relevant.

Historical paid orders must remain explainable even if current product compliance state later changes.

## 13. Fail-closed rules

Where required evidence is:

- missing;
- expired;
- contradicted;
- unverified;
- source rights unclear;
- supplier suspended;
- product recalled;

sellability should fail closed according to policy rather than silently treating unknown as approved.

## 14. External-source refresh

Immediately before implementing category/provider-specific behavior, re-verify from current official sources:

- applicable UK product regulations;
- product marking/traceability requirements;
- UK GDPR/privacy where evidence includes personal data;
- intellectual-property/content-use rules and provider terms;
- supplier/API/platform policies;
- recall/reporting obligations where relevant.

Do not encode volatile rules from memory.

## 15. E2E acceptance examples

Future E2E must prove at minimum:

1. product candidate with missing rights cannot auto-publish;
2. AI copy cannot invent a certification;
3. supplier-approved state does not bypass offer-level evidence;
4. expired evidence can remove sellability without deleting product history;
5. recalled product can be stopped while historical orders remain visible/auditable;
6. source images can be replaced without losing original provenance;
7. duplicate source data from multiple providers can converge while preserving distinct evidence trails;
8. provider connector failure cannot silently convert stale compliance data into current truth.

## 16. Gate rule

This document does NOT answer who legally bears every compliance/product-liability duty.

Gate B must resolve the commercial/legal responsibility split for:

- Marketplace Seller;
- Loadify Direct / Loadify-operated offer;
- Loadify Supplier-Fulfilled.

Only after that can compliance ownership be mapped into final schema, authorization and workflows.
